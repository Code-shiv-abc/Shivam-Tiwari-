## Project Overview

A fast-scaling company needed to grow its engineering team 4× across North America, Europe, and APAC — while maintaining quality, velocity, and culture.

## The Problem

The existing organization of 50 engineers was highly centralized. Knowledge silos were forming, the onboarding process took 8 weeks, and deployment velocity was suffering due to cross-team dependencies. There was no clear path for scaling the leadership pipeline, and team morale was beginning to dip as the monolithic architecture slowed everyone down.

## The Solution

I designed and implemented a distributed leadership model, restructuring the engineering department into cross-functional, autonomous pods aligned with core business domains.

### Architecture

We shifted from a monolithic team structure to a domain-driven design (DDD) organizational model. Each pod consisted of frontend, backend, QA, and product representation, reducing cross-team dependencies by 70%.

### Technical Decisions

- **Standardized tooling:** Adopted a golden path for services using a unified tech stack (Node.js/React).
- **Automated onboarding:** Built a self-serve developer portal that automated access provisioning and environment setup.
- **Metrics-driven management:** Implemented DORA metrics tracking across all pods to identify bottlenecks objectively.

## Challenges

The biggest challenge was cultural resistance to the new pod structure. Engineers were used to functional silos (e.g., all backend engineers reporting to a single backend manager). We overcame this by running a pilot program with two pods, demonstrating a 2x increase in deployment frequency and higher team satisfaction, which won over the rest of the organization.

## Tradeoffs

We traded some functional specialization for faster end-to-end delivery. While engineers had fewer opportunities to deep-dive into highly specialized technical silos, the business gained massive agility, and engineers reported feeling more connected to user impact.
